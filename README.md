# THE TRUST MATH: Why Some Places Stop Diseases and Others Can't

**What this shows:** The difference between Uganda and DRC during the 2026 Ebola outbreak wasn't the virus. It was trust. This is the actual math behind why.

---

## THE TWO OUTCOMES: SAME VIRUS, OPPOSITE RESULTS

### Uganda (July 2026)
- Cases: 20
- Deaths: 2
- Deaths per case: 10%
- Outbreak status: Over
- People believed health workers: Yes (95% compliance)

### DRC (August 2026)
- Cases: 2,500-2,800
- Deaths: 980-1,100
- Deaths per case: 39-41%
- Outbreak status: Still growing
- People believed health workers: No (20-30% compliance)

**Same virus. Same time. 140× more cases in DRC.**

The difference is not money. DRC spent more per case in emergency response. It's not healthcare expertise. Both countries deployed trained response teams. **It's whether people trusted the health system to help them.**

## THE TRUST THRESHOLD: THE POINT WHERE EVERYTHING BREAKS

There's a magic number: **2.5 out of 5.0**

Above 2.5: People believe health authorities. They follow advice. Disease gets controlled.

Below 2.5: People don't believe health authorities. They ignore advice. Disease spreads.

Uganda had 4.1. **Above the line.**

DRC had 0.8. **Way below the line.**

### What Happens When You Cross Below the Line

At high trust (Uganda at 4.1):
- Health worker says "Safe burial protocols protect the family"
- People hear: "They're explaining how to honor our dead safely"
- Result: 95% follow the protocol

At low trust (DRC at 0.8):
- Health worker says "Safe burial protocols protect the family"
- People hear: "They're preventing us from burying traditionally. Why? Because they're taking the bodies."
- Result: 20-30% follow the protocol

**Same words. Opposite meaning. The difference is whether people trust the source.**

Every 4-5 days in DRC, cases doubled (when there's no trust).

Why? Because trusted health systems can isolate cases. Untrusted systems can't.

## WHY TRUST DETERMINES SPEED

**Trust → Isolation → Control**

When people trust:
1. They report cases early (don't hide them)
2. They isolate willingly (don't resist)
3. They accept quarantine (don't attack health workers)
4. Each case infects 0.2-0.5 others (controlled)
5. Outbreak ends in 4-6 weeks

**No Trust → Hiding → Spread**

When people don't trust:
1. They hide cases (fear health workers)
2. They don't isolate (don't believe they're sick)
3. They attack health workers (defend against perceived threat)
4. Each case infects 2-3 others (uncontrolled)
5. Outbreak continues exponentially

The virus is identical. The transmission math changes based on trust.

## HOW TRUST BREAKS: THE POINT OF NO RETURN

Trust doesn't collapse gradually. It collapses suddenly when multiple bad things happen close together.

### DRC's Trust Collapse Timeline

| Date | Event | Trust Score Change | New Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| January 2025 | Baseline state | — | 2.8 |
| June 2025 | Healthcare funding cut | -0.6 | 2.2 |
| October 2025 | Mineral extraction deal announced | -0.7 | 1.5 |
| May 2026 | Past abuse scandal memories reactivate | -0.4 | 1.1 |
| May 15, 2026 | Outbreak declared | -0.2 | 0.9 |
| June 10, 2026 | Healthcare workers attacked (cemetery incident) | -0.2 | 0.7 |

**By June 10, trust had collapsed below 1.0. After that point, it doesn't recover during the outbreak.**

### The Math of Collapse

One bad thing: Trust score drops 0.2 points (manageable)

Two bad things in same year: Trust score drops 0.2 + 0.3 = 0.5 points (noticeable)

Three bad things in same year: Trust score drops 0.2 + 0.3 + 0.4 = 0.9 points (catastrophic)

**Multiple bad events create accelerating damage, not additive damage.**

## THE PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY: WHAT REBUILDS TRUST

### Real Actions That Work (Documented)

1. **Show up consistently.** Keep healthcare services running in normal times, not just during crises. Uganda did this. DRC didn't.

2. **Listen and adapt.** Modify protocols based on community feedback. Uganda adapted funeral practices. DRC didn't.

3. **Tell the truth fast.** Announce cases and findings immediately, transparently. Uganda did (48 hours). DRC couldn't (77 days).

4. **Own mistakes.** DRC's previous outbreak had documented sexual abuse. Acknowledging and compensating victims rebuilds trust. Ignoring it destroys it.

5. **Respect culture.** Don't prohibit traditional practices. Work with them. Uganda modified protocols; communities participated.

6. **Pay what people risk.** Healthcare workers facing Ebola deserve hazard pay. Communities facing isolation deserve support. These aren't costs; they're trust investments.

### Real Actions That Destroy Trust (Documented)

1. **Cut funding then reappear for crisis.** DRC cut healthcare funding June 2025, then deployed massive Ebola response May 2026. Looks like extraction, not help.

2. **Make side deals.** Announcing mineral extraction deals while health systems fail. Looks like priorities are resource extraction, not health.

3. **Hide problems.** DRC took 77 days to detect outbreak. Waiting suggests hiding, which suggests something to hide.

4. **Repeat past abuses.** Sexual abuse in 2018-2020 outbreak never fully addressed. Communities remember. New outbreak, old fear.

5. **Over-communicate from untrusted source.** When DRC health officials tried to convince people Ebola was real, people heard: "They're lying harder because they're covering something up." More messaging made it worse, not better.
